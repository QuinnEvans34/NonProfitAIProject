# Hope Connect — Legal Architecture v2 (Strict Safe Harbor)

**Purpose:** This document specifies the architectural commitments that support a strict Safe Harbor de-identification posture for Hope Connect. It exists to be evaluated by a second-opinion reviewer (and ultimately by qualified counsel) against the specific deficiencies identified in the prior round of review. Every architectural choice here is made to remove a specific objection the prior reviewer raised. We are not proposing creative engineering around HIPAA. We are accepting the constraints the prior review identified and asking whether the resulting architecture supports the position.

**Predecessor documents acknowledged as superseded:** `HOPE_CONNECT_PROPOSAL.md` (v1), `LEGAL_REVIEW_RESPONSE.md`. Both made claims that were not supported by their underlying architectures. This document replaces them.

**Legal posture stated as architectural intent, not as established fact.** All categorical statements about HIPAA applicability are subject to attorney review and second-opinion technical confirmation before being used in any public communication, marketing, contract, or sales material.

---

## Part 1 — Foundational position

Hope Connect (THC) is designed to receive only Safe-Harbor-de-identified information as defined at 45 CFR § 164.514(b)(2). Hope Connect does not perform de-identification on PHI; the tenant performs de-identification, and Hope Connect receives the result.

Specifically, Hope Connect's data plane is engineered such that:

- **No element of any of the eighteen identifier categories** in § 164.514(b)(2)(i)(A)–(R) is collected, transmitted to, or persisted by Hope Connect.
- **The Case Identifier**, the only opaque code Hope Connect holds, is generated and managed by the tenant under the requirements of § 164.514(c). Hope Connect does not hold any mapping that would permit re-identification.
- **No free-text input** from clients, case managers, or any other party is accepted by Hope Connect's API. The intake produces only structured responses (Likert values, predefined enums, predefined categorical tags). The case manager workspace permits only structured workflow actions (predefined status enums, predefined outcome tags). Narrative content of any kind lives exclusively on tenant-controlled infrastructure.
- **No date directly related to an individual** is stored at greater precision than year. This includes intake date, completion date, status-change dates, contact dates, and audit timestamps for events relating to the individual.
- **No geographic subdivision smaller than state** is stored. Tenant organizations are not associated with cities, counties, or ZIP codes in Hope Connect's database, and tenant names and identifiers do not encode location.
- **No client network metadata** (IP addresses, user-agents, cookies, headers, device identifiers) is received by Hope Connect or any of its subprocessors, because clients do not connect to Hope Connect infrastructure. Only authenticated tenant backends connect to Hope Connect's API, on a server-to-server basis.
- **No client authentication** occurs in Hope Connect. There are no client user accounts, no client-side OAuth, no client portals.
- **No browser-side join** of identity and case data occurs within Hope Connect's application code. Identity rendering and case data rendering occur in separately authenticated, separately origin'd iframes hosted within a tenant-controlled wrapper page. Hope Connect's frontend JavaScript never possesses identity data, OAuth tokens against tenant CRMs, or any cross-frame messaging carrying identity.
- **No notification infrastructure** of Hope Connect's transmits client information. All client-directed communication originates from tenant-controlled infrastructure. Hope Connect signals events to authenticated tenant systems using only the opaque Case Identifier and structured event payloads.

The intent of this architecture is to defend the position that Hope Connect does not create, receive, maintain, or transmit Protected Health Information as defined at 45 CFR § 160.103, and is therefore not a Business Associate of any tenant, regardless of any individual tenant's status as a Covered Entity.

This is a posture we are seeking to defend, not one we are asserting as established. Multiple aspects of this architecture are novel enough that we expect ongoing legal scrutiny.

## Part 2 — Inventory of data received

This inventory is the document the reviewer should evaluate first. If anything not listed here is received by Hope Connect, the posture fails.

### 2.1 What Hope Connect's API receives per intake

| Field | Type | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `case_id` | Opaque string (16 bytes from CSPRNG, base64url-encoded) | Generated by tenant backend | Validated under § 164.514(c). Tenant retains the re-identification mapping. |
| `tenant_id` | UUID | Hope Connect-assigned at tenant onboarding | Does not encode geography. |
| `submission_year` | Integer (e.g., 2026) | Tenant backend | Year only. No month, day, time. |
| `intake_template_id` | UUID | Hope Connect-managed | References the template version the tenant configured. |
| `screening_responses` | Array of `{ question_id, likert_value }` | Tenant backend (forwarded from intake) | Likert values are integers 1–5. No free text. |
| `categorical_responses` | Object of `{ field_id: enum_value }` | Tenant backend | All values from predefined enum lists. Examples: `need_category: "housing" \| "food" \| ...`, `urgency_window: "today" \| "this_week" \| ...`, `language: "en" \| "es" \| ...`. |
| `tag_selections` | Array of strings from predefined tag library | Tenant backend | Tags selected from Hope Connect's published catalog; no free-form tags. |
| `submission_signature` | Cryptographic signature | Tenant backend (using tenant-backend-only key) | Proves tenant backend signed the submission. Validates structural integrity, not de-identification quality. |

That is the exhaustive list. Hope Connect's API schema rejects any field outside this set. Hope Connect's API rejects any request whose body contains string fields exceeding a small length limit (e.g., 64 characters) outside the explicit `case_id`, `tenant_id`, and `submission_signature` fields, and rejects any string value not from the corresponding enum where applicable.

### 2.2 What Hope Connect's analyzer produces

The analyzer reads the structured inputs above and produces a structured output. No free-form narrative is generated.

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `severity_level` | Enum: `low`, `medium`, `high`, `crisis` | Selected from a fixed set. |
| `severity_score` | Integer 0–100 | Computed deterministically from severity_level + flag set. |
| `risk_flags` | Object of `{ flag_name: boolean }` | Predefined flag set: `housing_loss_imminent`, `food_insecurity`, `mental_health_concern`, etc. |
| `recommended_program_categories` | Array of enum values | Categories only (e.g., `emergency_food`, `eviction_prevention`). Specific programs are looked up by the tenant from the tenant's own local program catalog. |
| `recommended_followup_question_ids` | Array of IDs | Selected from a predefined library of follow-up questions; no generated text. |
| `primary_need_category` | Enum | Selected from predefined need categories. |
| `secondary_need_categories` | Array of enums | Same. |
| `urgency_window` | Enum: `today`, `this_week`, `this_month`, `planning_ahead` | Same. |

The analyzer does not produce: free-text summary, free-text AI comments, free-text rationale, free-text recommendations. Output is entirely structured.

The analyzer's underlying LLM call processes the structured inputs and is constrained to outputs from the enums above. This is implementable as a constrained generation call (e.g., JSON-schema-enforced generation with enum constraints) such that the LLM cannot produce free-form text.

### 2.3 What Hope Connect's database stores per intake

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| `case_id` | Per § 164.514(c), tenant-generated. |
| `tenant_id` | Opaque UUID. |
| `submission_year` | Integer year only. |
| `screening_responses` | Structured. |
| `categorical_responses` | Structured. |
| `tag_selections` | From predefined library. |
| `analyzer_output` | Structured per §2.2. |
| `current_status` | Enum value (e.g., `new`, `in_review`, `approved`, `contacted`, `closed`). |
| `status_history` | Ordered list of `{ status_enum, status_year, ordinal }` — year only, with ordinal for ordering within a year. |
| `analyzer_version` | Reference to which prompt/model version analyzed this intake. |

What is NOT stored:
- No client-related dates beyond year.
- No client IP, user-agent, headers, cookies.
- No case manager narrative or staff notes.
- No client name, email, phone, address, or any other identifier.
- No tenant city, county, ZIP, or geographic subdivision below state.
- No reference to specific programs the tenant ultimately referred the client to (that lives in the tenant's CRM).

### 2.4 What Hope Connect's API receives per case manager workflow action

| Field | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `case_id` | Per § 164.514(c). |
| `case_manager_id` | UUID of the tenant staff user. | Case manager email is stored at user creation; not transmitted per-action. |
| `action_type` | Enum: `status_change`, `override_severity`, `request_reanalysis`, `archive`, etc. |
| `action_payload` | Structured per action type (e.g., `{ from_status, to_status }`). No free text. |
| `action_year` | Year only. |
| `action_ordinal` | Per-case ordinal for ordering. |

What is NOT received: timestamps of action, IP of case manager, narrative justification.

### 2.5 What Hope Connect's logs contain

Application logs, access logs, error logs, and audit logs contain:

- Case IDs (per § 164.514(c)).
- Tenant IDs.
- Case manager user IDs.
- Action types and structured payloads.
- HTTP status codes and endpoints (without query parameters that could contain identifiers).
- Year-truncated timestamps.

Logs do not contain: IP addresses (stripped at the ALB layer before logs are written), user-agent strings, cookies, free-text body content, dates at greater than year precision, error stack traces that include user-supplied content.

This is enforced both by application code and by log-shipping configuration to Sentry / observability vendors.

## Part 3 — Architectural specification

### 3.1 The data flow

```
1. CLIENT INTAKE (entirely on tenant infrastructure)
   ├─ Client visits tenant.example.org/get-help
   ├─ Tenant serves intake form from tenant's own infrastructure
   ├─ Form contains ONLY structured input:
   │     • Likert questions (1–5 scale)
   │     • Categorical dropdowns (selected from predefined enums)
   │     • Predefined tag checkboxes
   │     • NO "tell us about your situation" free-text field
   │     • NO comment boxes
   │     • NO text input of any kind
   ├─ Client submits to tenant backend
   └─ All client metadata (IP, user-agent, etc.) remains on tenant infrastructure

2. TENANT BACKEND PROCESSING (tenant infrastructure)
   ├─ Receives structured submission from client
   ├─ Generates Case ID using CSPRNG (16 bytes, base64url)
   ├─ Stores in tenant's CRM: { case_id, client_name, contact_info, intake_date, ... }
   │   The tenant retains the re-identification mapping as the entity performing
   │   de-identification under § 164.514(c).
   ├─ Constructs the de-identified payload for Hope Connect:
   │     • case_id
   │     • tenant_id (Hope Connect-assigned)
   │     • submission_year (truncated to year)
   │     • screening_responses (structured)
   │     • categorical_responses (structured, enum values only)
   │     • tag_selections (from predefined library)
   ├─ Tenant backend signs the payload using a tenant-backend-only signing key
   │   (key never enters the browser, never enters Hope Connect)
   ├─ POSTs to Hope Connect API over server-to-server TLS

3. HOPE CONNECT API (AWS, behind Cloudflare)
   ├─ Accepts traffic from authenticated tenant backends only
   ├─ Validates signature
   ├─ Validates schema:
   │     • Every field matches the schema in §2.1
   │     • Any string field outside case_id/tenant_id/signature is bounded length
   │     • Any string field is from the corresponding enum
   │     • Rejects any unexpected field
   ├─ Persists the structured intake record (per §2.3)
   ├─ Enqueues the analyzer job

4. HOPE CONNECT ANALYZER (cloud LLM via Anthropic / equivalent)
   ├─ Reads structured intake record
   ├─ Calls LLM with constrained output schema (JSON schema with enums only)
   ├─ LLM produces structured AnalysisResult (per §2.2)
   ├─ Validates schema
   ├─ Persists analyzer output
   ├─ Optionally publishes an event to a tenant-registered webhook URL with the
   │   Case ID and event type (no client information)

5. CASE MANAGER WORKSPACE (isolated iframes on tenant-controlled wrapper page)
   ├─ Case manager opens tenant.example.org/cases/<case_id> (tenant-controlled page)
   ├─ The wrapper page contains TWO iframes:
   │
   │   IFRAME A: identity.example.org/case/<case_id>
   │     • Served from tenant or tenant-delegated origin
   │     • Authenticates against the tenant's identity store
   │     • Displays client name, phone, email, contact history, narrative notes
   │     • All these data live in tenant's CRM
   │
   │   IFRAME B: hopeconnect.app/case/<case_id>
   │     • Served from Hope Connect origin
   │     • Authenticates against Hope Connect's auth (Clerk)
   │     • Displays severity, screening responses, analyzer output, status controls
   │     • Hope Connect's JS never sees what IFRAME A contains
   │
   ├─ The two iframes do NOT communicate. No postMessage carrying identity.
   │   No shared session. No shared analytics. No shared error capture.
   ├─ Case manager visually reads both, makes their judgment, takes their action
   ├─ Status updates and structured workflow actions submitted via IFRAME B
   ├─ Narrative notes and identity updates submitted via IFRAME A
   └─ Each iframe communicates only with its own origin's backend

6. TENANT-OPERATED NOTIFICATIONS
   ├─ Hope Connect API publishes structured events to tenant webhook (Case ID + event type only)
   ├─ Tenant backend receives event
   ├─ Tenant backend joins event to tenant's CRM identity for the Case ID
   ├─ Tenant backend sends notifications via tenant's own infrastructure
   │   (tenant's Twilio account, tenant's SendGrid, tenant's anything)
   └─ Hope Connect has no subprocessor relationship with Twilio, SendGrid,
       or any notification provider — those are tenant subprocessors.
```

### 3.2 The Case Identifier under § 164.514(c)

The Case Identifier is structured to satisfy the requirements of § 164.514(c).

**The tenant, as the entity performing de-identification of the underlying PHI:**

1. Assigns the Case Identifier at intake time using a CSPRNG. The identifier is not derived from any information about the individual. The identifier does not encode date, demographic, geographic, or any other individual attribute.
2. Maintains the re-identification mapping in the tenant's own CRM, never disclosed to Hope Connect.
3. Discloses to Hope Connect only the Case Identifier alongside the de-identified record per §2.1, and never discloses the re-identification key or mechanism to Hope Connect.
4. Limits the Case Identifier's use to the disclosure to Hope Connect and to the tenant's own internal lookup.

**Hope Connect:**

1. Receives the Case Identifier as the only persistent linking value for the de-identified record.
2. Uses the Case Identifier only for: (a) database record retrieval, (b) workflow lookup, (c) per-tenant aggregate analytics with cell-size suppression, (d) referencing the case in webhook events sent back to the tenant.
3. Does not disclose the Case Identifier to any third party other than the tenant who created it.
4. Does not attempt to re-identify the individual associated with any Case Identifier, and does not maintain any mapping (in any system, including in case manager memory, structured fields, or external systems) that would permit re-identification.
5. Contractually commits in the Master Subscription Agreement to all of the above.

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether this structure satisfies § 164.514(c).

### 3.3 The isolated iframe architecture for the case manager workspace

This is the critical architectural commitment that resolves the prior "browser join" objection.

```
Browser top-level page (tenant-controlled origin: tenant.example.org)
│
├─ <iframe src="https://tenant-identity.example.org/case/X" sandbox="...">
│     This iframe contains tenant identity UI:
│     • client name, phone, email, address
│     • contact history
│     • narrative notes (created and read here)
│     • case manager writes notes that go to tenant CRM
│     Origin: tenant-controlled. Communicates only with tenant CRM.
│
└─ <iframe src="https://hopeconnect.app/case/X" sandbox="...">
      This iframe contains Hope Connect case data UI:
      • severity, score, screening responses
      • analyzer output (recommended categories, follow-up question IDs)
      • status controls (structured)
      • workflow actions
      Origin: Hope Connect. Communicates only with Hope Connect API.

The top-level page does not exchange data between the iframes.
The iframes are sandboxed:
  - sandbox="allow-scripts allow-same-origin allow-forms"
  - explicitly does NOT have allow-top-navigation, allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox
  - postMessage between iframes is structurally prevented by origin restrictions
  - no shared analytics scripts
  - no shared error reporting
  - no session replay
```

**Hope Connect's JavaScript:**

- Loads from `hopeconnect.app` only.
- Communicates only with `api.hopeconnect.app`.
- Never receives any OAuth token against any tenant CRM.
- Never receives any client name, phone, email, address.
- Never receives any data from the tenant identity iframe via postMessage, BroadcastChannel, localStorage sharing, cookies, or any other channel.
- The Hope Connect-origin script accesses only structured Case Data from Hope Connect's API.

**Tenant identity iframe:**

- Loads from `tenant-identity.example.org` (or equivalent tenant-controlled origin).
- Communicates only with tenant identity backend.
- Holds any necessary OAuth tokens for the tenant's CRM.
- Renders identity data and submits identity updates.
- Never sends data to the Hope Connect iframe.

**The "join":**

- Purely visual: the case manager sees both iframes rendered side-by-side and reads them with their eyes.
- The case manager forms the mental link between the case data and the identity.
- This visual juxtaposition is a human cognitive operation, not a software join.
- Hope Connect's software never possesses both halves of the link.

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether this architecture resolves the prior objection about Hope Connect's JavaScript processing identifiable health information.

### 3.4 Notification architecture

Hope Connect does not send notifications to clients or to non-Hope-Connect-staff under any circumstances. The notification path is:

1. Hope Connect's API publishes structured events to a tenant-registered webhook URL.
2. The event payload contains: `{ case_id, event_type, structured_data }`. No client information.
3. The tenant's webhook handler receives the event on tenant infrastructure.
4. The tenant's handler joins the event to the tenant's CRM identity by Case ID.
5. The tenant's infrastructure (tenant's Twilio account, tenant's SendGrid account, tenant's notification system) issues the actual notification.

Hope Connect's only outbound communications are:

- API responses to authenticated tenant backends (containing only Case Data per §2.3).
- Webhook events to tenant URLs (containing only Case ID and structured event data).
- Authenticated emails to Hope Connect staff users (case managers, tenant admins, Hope Connect master admins) at email addresses captured by Clerk during user setup. These emails reference Case IDs only, never client names or other identifiers.

Twilio, SendGrid, Postmark, and similar notification providers are NOT Hope Connect's subprocessors and do not appear in Hope Connect's subprocessor list. They are tenant subprocessors and the tenant assumes the responsibility for their HIPAA status.

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether this notification architecture removes Twilio (and equivalents) from Hope Connect's subprocessor scope.

### 3.5 The intake form: structured-only

Hope Connect's published intake template specification permits the following input types:

- **Likert scale** (integer 1–5)
- **Single-select enum** (one value from a predefined list)
- **Multi-select enum** (multiple values from a predefined list)
- **Predefined tag selection** (boolean checkbox against a tag from the published library)

The specification does NOT permit:

- Free-text input
- Number input outside the Likert range
- Date input
- File upload
- Any field type that could carry narrative content

This is enforced at three layers:

1. **Hope Connect's intake template schema** rejects any template version that includes a non-structured field.
2. **The tenant's intake form code** built from a Hope Connect template uses only structured input components.
3. **Hope Connect's API** rejects any submission containing string values not in the allowed enum, or any field type outside the schema.

The trade-off: clients cannot describe their situation in their own words to Hope Connect. They can describe it to the tenant's staff via the tenant's own channels (phone, in-person, the tenant's own intake form for narrative if the tenant chooses). That narrative lives entirely in the tenant CRM, not in Hope Connect.

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether this resolves the prior objection about scrubbing-quality being insufficient.

### 3.6 Date handling

All dates directly related to an individual are stored at year-only precision in Hope Connect's database:

- `submission_year` — the year the tenant forwarded the de-identified record to Hope Connect.
- `status_change_year` — the year a status transition occurred (per status history entry).
- `analyzer_year` — the year the analyzer produced an output for a case.

No month, day, hour, or finer precision is stored.

For operational ordering within a year (e.g., to display intakes in order received), Hope Connect uses a per-tenant per-year ordinal counter rather than wall-clock time. The ordinal is monotonically increasing within a (tenant, year) but conveys no information about the wall-clock time of any individual event.

For SLA tracking, Hope Connect can compute coarse SLA buckets (e.g., "responded within first ordinal third of year") but does not store actual elapsed days or hours.

Hope Connect system-level metrics that are NOT individual-related (e.g., total API request count per hour, system uptime) can use precise timestamps because they are not tied to individuals.

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether year-only precision satisfies the date requirement of § 164.514(b)(2)(i)(C).

### 3.7 Geographic handling

Hope Connect's tenant model does not encode geography below state level.

- **Tenant identifiers** (UUIDs) do not encode geography.
- **Tenant display names** in Hope Connect's admin tooling are operationally chosen but contractually constrained not to encode geographic specificity. "Freebird Foundation" is acceptable; "Hope Connect Charlotte" or "Mecklenburg County Services" are not.
- **No tenant.city, tenant.county, tenant.zip, tenant.address** fields exist in Hope Connect's schema.
- **No client geographic data** of any kind is collected from clients (no ZIP code question, no county question, no city question in the intake template).
- **Aggregate reports** are produced at state level only or at fully tenant-pooled level. Per-tenant reports show only that tenant's data without geographic decomposition.

For operational purposes (a case manager from Tenant A needing to filter to Tenant A's cases), the filtering is done by `tenant_id` (opaque UUID), not by geography.

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether this resolves the prior objection about tenant geography.

## Part 4 — Subprocessor analysis

For each subprocessor, the data flowing to it and the rationale for non-BAA status:

| Subprocessor | What it receives | BAA required? | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cloudflare** | Server-to-server TLS traffic from tenant backends to Hope Connect API. IPs are tenant infrastructure IPs, not client IPs. | No | Does not receive client metadata. Does not receive PHI. Receives only Case IDs (per § 164.514(c)) and structured de-identified data. |
| **AWS (compute, RDS, S3, ALB)** | Same as above, plus storage of de-identified Case Data per §2.3. | No (BAA available regardless and recommended for general hygiene; not legally required) | Does not receive PHI. |
| **Anthropic (analyzer LLM)** | Structured Case Data per §2.3 only. No free text. Receives nothing identifying. | No | Does not receive PHI. Cannot generate identifying output (constrained generation to enums). |
| **Clerk (auth)** | Staff user emails (case managers, tenant admins, Hope Connect admins). No client identities. | No | Staff identity is not PHI. Does not see Case IDs. |
| **Sentry / Better Stack (observability)** | Structured application logs, scrubbed of client metadata. Case IDs may appear in operational logs. | No | Case IDs are permitted under § 164.514(c). Logs contain no narrative, no IPs, no client data. |
| **Stripe (billing)** | Tenant organization billing info. No client data. | No | Tenant billing is not PHI. |
| **GitHub (source control, CI)** | Source code only. No production data. | No | No PHI ever in repo. |

The following are NOT Hope Connect subprocessors (they are tenant subprocessors, operated by the tenant under the tenant's responsibilities):

- Twilio (tenant's, if tenant chooses to use for notifications)
- SendGrid / Postmark for client-directed email (tenant's)
- The tenant's CRM (Salesforce, Apricot, HubSpot, Airtable, Google Sheets)
- The tenant's identity iframe origin
- The tenant's intake portal hosting

The reviewer is asked to confirm whether this subprocessor list is complete and whether each non-BAA determination is correct.

## Part 5 — Point-by-point response to prior critique

For each item from the prior reviewer's critique, the resolution under this architecture.

### Critique item 1: Day-level dates are prohibited

**Architecture v2:** Year-only dates throughout. Per §3.6.

### Critique item 2: Rejection after receipt is still receipt

**Architecture v2:** Hope Connect does not accept any free-text input from any source. The Hope Connect API schema does not include any free-text field. The intake template specification prohibits non-structured fields. There is therefore no need for server-side scrubbing because there is nothing to scrub. The defense moves from "we scrub before storing" to "we structurally cannot receive what we would need to scrub."

### Critique item 3: An imperfect scrubber cannot guarantee de-identification

**Architecture v2:** No scrubber is necessary. No automated detection is necessary. The architecture forbids free text at the input layer. The contradiction between "perfect scrubbing" and "no scrubber is perfect" is resolved by removing the scrubber and the free text entirely.

### Critique item 4: Submission signing proves almost nothing

**Architecture v2:** Signing is performed by the tenant backend using a key that never enters the browser. The tenant backend has performed structural validation (every field is in the schema, every value is from the corresponding enum). The signing key proves that the tenant backend, which is the entity performing de-identification, signed the payload. The signature attests that the tenant performed its de-identification responsibilities. It is not a proxy for scrubbing quality because scrubbing is not needed.

### Critique item 5: The Case ID issue is not "mooted"

**Architecture v2:** § 164.514(c) is acknowledged as the operative rule and the architecture is specifically structured to satisfy it. Per §3.2: tenant generates the Case ID as the entity performing de-identification; tenant retains the re-identification mapping; tenant discloses only the Case ID alongside the de-identified record; Hope Connect does not disclose the Case ID externally and does not attempt re-identification; all of this is contractually committed in the MSA.

### Critique item 6: The browser join still gives Hope software access

**Architecture v2:** Resolved via isolated iframes per §3.3. Hope Connect's JavaScript loads from the Hope Connect origin only, communicates only with Hope Connect's API, never holds any OAuth token against any tenant CRM, never receives identity data via postMessage or any other channel. The "join" between identity and case data is purely visual within the case manager's perception, not a software operation. Hope Connect's software does not process identifiable health information at any point.

### Critique item 7: Expert Determination cannot wait

**Architecture v2:** Under strict Safe Harbor, no Expert Determination is required because the architecture removes the elements that would necessitate it (day-level dates, narrative text, geography below state). We may voluntarily commission an Expert Determination as additional documentation of the residual re-identification risk on the structured data combinations, but it is not required as a precondition to launch under this architecture.

### Critique item 8: Tenant location remains problematic

**Architecture v2:** Per §3.7, Hope Connect's data plane stores no geographic subdivision below state. Tenant names, identifiers, and metadata do not encode geographic specificity. Aggregate reports are state-level or fully pooled.

### Critique item 9: The local model specification is unrealistic

**Architecture v2:** No local model is used. No browser-side LLM scrubbing exists in this architecture because there is no free text to scrub. The prior specification of Phi-3.5-mini is moot and removed.

### Critique item 10: Subprocessor conclusions are premature; Twilio is questionable

**Architecture v2:** Per §3.4, Twilio (and all client-directed notification providers) are tenant subprocessors, not Hope Connect's. Hope Connect's only outbound communications are: API responses to authenticated tenant backends, webhooks to tenant URLs containing only Case IDs and structured event types, and emails to authenticated Hope Connect staff users referencing only Case IDs. No client information is sent through any subprocessor.

## Part 6 — What this architecture costs in product terms

This document is deliberately not optimistic about the product implications. The reviewer should understand the trade-offs:

**Removed from the product:**

- "Tell me about your situation" free-text intake field. Clients describe their situation only through structured responses.
- Per-section comment fields on the screener. No narrative input.
- AI Comments as free-text bullet points. Replaced with structured tagged comments from a predefined library.
- AI-generated narrative summary for case managers. Replaced with structured analyzer output that the case manager reads from structured fields.
- Staff notes in Hope Connect. Notes live in the tenant CRM only.
- Hope Connect-generated client-facing language. Replaced with tenant-controlled messaging.
- Hope Connect-side program directory specifics. The tenant maintains a local program catalog mapped from Hope Connect's category outputs.
- Cross-tenant geographic analytics (e.g., "needs in Mecklenburg County"). Only state-level and tenant-pooled analytics are possible.
- Day-level or finer trend analytics for individual cases or per-individual SLAs. Only year-bucketed analytics.
- Integrated case manager UI that displays identity and case data as one interface. Replaced with side-by-side isolated iframes in a tenant-hosted wrapper.
- The browser-side scrubber SDK we previously specified. Not needed and not built.

**Retained:**

- The structured screener (Likert, categorical).
- The analyzer (constrained to structured output).
- The severity-and-help-score deterministic computation.
- The rule-based crisis floor (operating on structured signals, not free text).
- Multi-tenant infrastructure.

## Part 7 — Residual risks explicitly acknowledged

We do not claim this architecture eliminates all risk. The following remain and are documented for the reviewer's attention.

**R1 — Combination re-identification on structured data.** Even with names, dates, geography, and narrative all removed, the combination of Likert responses, categorical answers, and tag selections could in principle identify individuals in small tenant populations. Safe Harbor's catch-all at § 164.514(b)(2)(ii) — "the covered entity does not have actual knowledge that the information could be used alone or in combination with other information to identify an individual" — is a real condition we have to be able to attest to. Mitigation: cell-size suppression on all aggregates, no individual record disclosure outside Hope Connect, contractual commitment not to attempt re-identification.

**R2 — Tenant non-compliance with the architecture.** If a tenant violates the architecture (e.g., includes a free-text field in their intake form, encodes geography in their tenant name, fails to perform de-identification before submission), PHI could enter Hope Connect despite the architecture. Mitigation: Hope Connect's API rejects non-conforming submissions; intake template specification is published and validated; MSA requires tenant compliance; tenant audit rights.

**R3 — The constrained analyzer may be less useful.** The product value of the analyzer is reduced when it can only output enum values. Some of the qualitative richness of the original analyzer is lost. This is a product cost we accept.

**R4 — Iframe isolation is not absolute.** The case manager workspace is engineered to prevent cross-iframe data flow, but sophisticated attacks or misconfiguration could in principle bridge the two. Mitigation: sandbox attributes, origin restrictions, no shared scripts, security review of the wrapper page.

**R5 — Case ID misuse.** If Hope Connect were ever to disclose Case IDs to third parties or use them for purposes outside the §3.2 list, the § 164.514(c) compliance would be compromised. Mitigation: code-level restriction on Case ID disclosure, audit log of all Case ID accesses, MSA contractual commitment.

**R6 — Novel legal posture.** This architecture is not common in the social-services SaaS market. We cannot rely on case law or industry-standard sign-offs. Mitigation: attorney review, second-opinion technical review, documented architecture, willingness to revise as guidance evolves.

## Part 9 — What we are explicitly committing to

For clarity, the architectural commitments below are non-negotiable parts of the v2 architecture. Any deviation is treated as a violation of the legal posture.

1. Hope Connect's API will not accept any field outside the schema in §2.1.
2. Hope Connect's intake template specification will not permit non-structured input types.
3. Hope Connect's database will not store any date directly related to an individual at greater than year precision.
4. Hope Connect's database will not store any geographic subdivision smaller than state.
5. Hope Connect's database will not store any free-text field (no narrative, no comments, no notes).
6. Hope Connect's frontend JavaScript will not handle any tenant CRM OAuth token or any client identity.
7. Hope Connect's case manager workspace will be deployed only as the Hope-Connect-origin iframe within a tenant-hosted wrapper, never as a standalone page that case managers visit directly.
8. Hope Connect's outbound communications will not include any client-related information beyond Case IDs.
9. Hope Connect will not enter subprocessor relationships with client-directed notification providers.
10. Hope Connect's MSA will impose corresponding responsibilities on tenants for de-identification before submission and for non-disclosure of the re-identification mapping.

These commitments are technical and enforceable in code, not just contractual.
